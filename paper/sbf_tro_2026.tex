\title{RapidBoxForest: Volumetric Reuse with Failure-Closed Route Status for Manipulator Motion Planning}

\author{Anonymous Authors}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Repeated manipulator planning queries in semi-static workcells require reusable
kinematic evidence without reusing scene-local validity. RapidBoxForest (\rbf{})
couples an axis-aligned joint-space box graph with a Lifelong Envelope Cache
Tree (LECT). LECT gates obstacle-independent direct-store replay by semantic
identity; the evaluated snapshot assumes a co-generated verified store. \rbf{}
derives link envelopes and refreshes scene labels and edges. Descriptor-aligned
refinement and zero-gap exact-union coalescing construct a budgeted overlap
graph with typed corridor transitions. A returned path is certificate-backed
only when complete current-scene witnesses cover it; reported paths lack this
provenance and are audited at a fixed step. A Shelf+IIWA compound comparison
gives median builds of 0.171~s for warm affine-arithmetic interval forward
kinematics (IFK-AA) replay and 2.770~s for on-demand five-level hierarchical
interval forward kinematics (HIFK-5); the 1008.6-s prewarm is excluded. At
method-specific points passing all catalog audits, \rbf{}'s query-matched
median path-length ratios span 1.00--1.05; bidirectional rapidly-exploring
random tree (RRT-Connect) and Batch Informed Trees (BIT*) span 1.02--1.36 and
1.04--1.07. Amortizing Build over ten queries lowers \rbf{}'s mean
build-plus-task diagnostic by 70--79\% relative to one query. At 0.50~rad,
HIFK-5 reduces median endpoint volume by 76\% at 9.8 times the IFK-AA
construction cost.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Motion planning, robot manipulators, multi-query planning, collision checking,
configuration-space decomposition, link envelopes.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Manipulators repeatedly solve related queries in semi-static workcells, where
kinematics stay fixed but start--goal pairs and local clutter change. A reusable
planner must amortize free-space construction, separate persistent kinematic
evidence from scene-local validity, and track whether every returned segment
retains conservative current-scene support.

Existing methods occupy different reuse regimes. Probabilistic roadmaps,
experience graphs, and safety certificates reuse connectivity, paths, or
clearance neighborhoods, whereas tree and batch planners typically construct
query-local search structures~\cite{kavraki1996probabilistic,
bialkowski2016safety,coleman2015experience,karaman2011sampling}. Certified
polyhedral decompositions, safe-box planners, and Graphs of Convex Sets (GCS)
methods instead make collision-free regions explicit~\cite{dai2024certified,
marcucci2024fastboxes,marcucci2024shortest,morozov2026multiquery}.
This motivates the design question: how can explicit regions persist across
queries and obstacle revisions while only identity-matched kinematic evidence
is reused and every obstacle-dependent label is refreshed?
Table~\ref{tab:related_qualitative_comparison} positions the proposed
RapidBoxForest (\rbf{}) and Lifelong Envelope Cache Tree (LECT) against
representative planning families by reusable state and validation
responsibility.

\rbf{} addresses this design point by growing reusable axis-aligned
configuration-space (\cspace{}) boxes around scene-relevant seeds, validating
them with envelopes of swept rounded links, and connecting accepted boxes in a
typed corridor graph. Axis alignment trades general convex-region expressivity
for dyadic addressing, inexpensive overlap/contact tests, and local
invalidation. We evaluate this trade-off through build cost, fixed-step audit
outcomes, path quality, and envelope tightness.

\paragraph{Problem statement}
Let $\Q\subset\Real^n$ be the joint-limit box of a fixed
\(n\)-degree-of-freedom manipulator, and let $\{\calO^{(r)}\}$ denote workspace
obstacle revisions under a fixed validation descriptor $\delta$. For revision
$r$, $\Cfree(\calO^{(r)})\subseteq\Q$ contains the configurations free of
active-link--obstacle collision under \Cref{ass:collision_model}, and each query
supplies start and goal configurations $(q_s,q_g)\in\Q^2$. \rbf{} constructs a finite family of
policy-accepted \cspace{} boxes and their adjacency graph for each revision.
Across revisions, only obstacle-independent endpoint evidence whose
joint-region key and recorded endpoint-evidence identity fields match may be
replayed; the current $\delta$ governs link-envelope derivation and scene
validation, while box labels and graph connectivity are recomputed for
$\calO^{(r)}$. Within a revision, this query-agnostic scene
structure is reused across start--goal queries, while query-endpoint attachment and repair
remain query-specific. The target is a volumetric, auditable structure rather
than an exhaustive decomposition of $\Cfree(\calO^{(r)})$.

\paragraph{Reuse and certificate semantics}
Building on classical endpoint-to-link enclosures, \rbf{} contributes a
reusable planning architecture with versioned evidence identity,
scene-local relabeling, typed transitions, and whole-route provenance. We
distinguish \emph{geometry
evidence} (descriptor-keyed endpoint enclosures that may persist), \emph{scene
structure} (current boxes, labels, and graph edges), and \emph{returned-path
status} (certificate-backed only if current-scene containment and separation
witnesses cover every segment; otherwise validation-required).
We call accepted planning cells \emph{policy-accepted regions} and reserve
\emph{certified free-volume regions} for conservatively validated boxes or
finite-cover corridors. \Cref{tab:claim_boundary} states the full boundary.

The paper makes three contributions that jointly realize this contract:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{Two-level reuse with scene-local validation.}
  \rbf{} reuses a typed box graph across queries within one scene. Across
  compatible revisions, LECT defines exact identity gates for
  obstacle-independent endpoint-evidence replay; \rbf{} requires obstacle
  labels and graph connectivity to be recomputed under the current scene.
  \item \emph{Budgeted typed-region construction.}
  Descriptor-aligned refinement and zero-gap exact-union coalescing build a
  reusable certified-free (\(\mathrm{CertFree}\)) overlap subgraph; a corridor layer augments it
  with typed tolerance-gap, segment, repair-witness, and finite-cover oriented
  bounding box (OBB) edges.
  \item \emph{Failure-closed route-status contract.}
  We formalize whole-route status from current-scene provenance: every endpoint
  attachment, bridge, repair, shortcut, or other segment must retain containment
  and separation support; otherwise the entire return is validation-required.
\end{enumerate}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:leaf_sweep_refine} specifies reusable overlap-subgraph
construction; \Cref{sec:forest_graph} and \Cref{def:path_witness} define typed
corridor augmentation and whole-route status; and
\Cref{prop:scene_safe_replay} gives the conditional safety contract for
identity-matched endpoint-evidence replay.
Evaluation questions Q1--Q4 quantify replay, isolate envelope costs, and compare
multi-query path-quality operating points.

Fig.~\ref{fig:linkiaabb} summarizes the method framework. Link-interval
computation creates reusable geometry evidence. \rbf{} growth recomputes scene
labels and graph connectivity for the current obstacles, while LECT replay
changes construction cost without changing validation responsibility.
Sections~\ref{sec:link_envelopes}--\ref{sec:exp} make these blocks precise and
then evaluate them.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Region-based planners make collision-free volume an explicit planning object.
IRIS (Iterative Regional Inflation by Semidefinite programming) constructs
convex free-space regions, while configuration-space IRIS (C-IRIS) rigorously
certifies \cspace{} polytopes~\cite{deits2015computing,dai2024certified}.
Probabilistic nonlinear-programming and sampling-based extensions, including
graphics processing unit (GPU) implementations, target faster region
construction under probabilistic collision guarantees~\cite{petersen2023growing,
werner2025superfast,werner2026faster}. Visibility-graph clique covers seek
compact approximate covers~\cite{werner2024approximating}. GCS optimizes paths
through supplied region graphs, whereas GCS* searches implicit
GCSs~\cite{marcucci2023motion,marcucci2024shortest,
chewchia2026gcsstar}. \rbf{} instead uses axis-aligned \cspace{} boxes and a
budgeted cover, prioritizing addressing, replay, and scene relabeling over
general convex-region expressivity.

\paragraph{Closest methods}
Marcucci \etal{} preprocess intersections in a supplied axis-aligned safe-box
collection and then generate smooth paths within its union, whereas Morozov
\etal{} precompute a cost-to-go lower bound for repeated online queries on a
supplied GCS~\cite{marcucci2024fastboxes,morozov2026multiquery}. Both begin
from a supplied structure; \rbf{} also constructs its budgeted box collection
and specifies cross-revision persistence. Safety certificates cache clearance
neighborhoods; dynamic roadmaps update affected graph elements; Ashur \etal{} index
swept-volume approximations of roadmap vertices and edges to refresh validity
after discrete obstacle motions; Thunder repairs graph experience; and soft
subdivision conservatively classifies
cells~\cite{bialkowski2016safety,leven2002framework,ashur2026spite,
coleman2015experience,wang2015soft}. \rbf{} instead combines versioned joint-box
endpoint evidence and current-descriptor link-envelope derivation with
scene-local relabeling and failure-closed whole-route status.

\paragraph{Endpoint bounds for continuous collision detection}
\label{sec:related_endpoint_bounds}
Swept-volume and interval-kinematics methods supply the enclosure primitives
used here. Convex-hull generator bounds and capsule-radius lifts provide
swept-set enclosures~\cite{blackmore1992swept,abdel2006swept}. The Proximity
Query Package (PQP), Gilbert--Johnson--Keerthi (GJK) algorithm, and Flexible
Collision Library (FCL) provide collision-query primitives, while interval
Denavit--Hartenberg propagation bounds articulated
kinematics~\cite{gottschalk1996obbtree,larsen1999fast,gilbert1988fast,bergen1999gjk,
pan2012fcl,moore2009introduction,jaulin2001applied,snyder1992interval,
merlet2004solving,merlet2009interval}. Redon \etal{} combine such components for
articulated-body continuous collision detection over time
steps~\cite{redon2004ccd_avatar,redon2005fast_artic}; Taylor-model and safety methods
use related swept-sphere bounds~\cite{zhang2007taylor,schwarzer2005adaptive,
corrales2011ssl}. These works provide the enclosure primitives used by \rbf{}.
Building on them, \rbf{} keys joint-box endpoint evidence by an exact versioned
identity, replays only matching records across revisions, derives link envelopes
under the current validation descriptor, and recomputes current-scene
separation.

\paragraph{Graph reuse}
Reusable graph planners amortize effort across related queries. A probabilistic
roadmap (PRM) amortizes a collision-checked roadmap across multiple queries,
whereas LazyPRM delays node and edge checks until they lie on a candidate
path~\cite{kavraki1996probabilistic,bohlin2000path}; adaptive cell methods split
cells until they obtain a searchable accepted-cell
graph~\cite{lozanoperez1983spatial,brooks1985subdivision,wang2015soft}. \rbf{}
instead grows a budgeted overlapping cover; its vertices are policy-accepted
regions, and transitions without containment provenance remain
validation-required.

\paragraph{Sampling and refinement}
Among rapidly-exploring random tree (RRT) variants, RRT-Connect is a
bidirectional single-query planner. Batch Informed Trees (BIT*) is
anytime and asymptotically optimal over growing sample batches; Fast Marching
Tree (FMT*) is asymptotically optimal over a predetermined random sample
set~\cite{kuffner2000rrt,gammell2015batch,janson2015fmt}. The Open Motion
Planning Library (OMPL) provides benchmarking
infrastructure~\cite{sucan2012ompl,moll2015benchmarking}; trajectory optimizers refine supplied
paths~\cite{ratliff2009chomp}. \rbf{} adds reusable volumetric context before
repair, optimization, and final audit; it is not a single-query optimal planner.

\noindent\emph{Synthesis.}
Methods built on supplied regions and caches conditioned on obstacles retain
different state. \rbf{} uses a two-level split: a typed box graph is reused across
queries within one scene, whereas only versioned, obstacle-independent endpoint
geometry persists across compatible revisions. Current-scene labels and
adjacency are rebuilt, and retained typed witnesses separate search
connectivity from certificate-backed connectivity.

\section{Link Interval Envelopes}\label{sec:link_envelopes}

This section defines endpoint and rounded-link envelopes for axis-aligned joint
boxes and finite-cover OBB corridors; caching and graph construction follow in
Sections~\ref{sec:lect} and~\ref{sec:forest}. For a joint region $I\subseteq\Q$,
$E_k(I)$ bounds endpoint $k$'s trajectory, and $B_\ell(I)$ bounds the complete
sweep of rounded link $\ell$:
\[
I \mapsto \{E_k(I)\} \mapsto \{B_\ell(I)\}.
\]
Axis-aligned boxes are the default primitive; local OBB corridors are
certificate-eligible only after the finite-cover validation below. Geometry
becomes certificate evidence only after conservative current-scene separation;
empirical endpoint sources remain filters under \Cref{tab:claim_boundary}.

\begin{assumption}[Collision model for certificates]
\label{ass:collision_model}
The certificate model covers only workspace-obstacle collision between active
rounded links and $\mathcal{O}$; joint limits are enforced separately by
$I\subseteq\Q$. The evaluated implementation represents $\mathcal{O}$ as a
finite union of closed workspace axis-aligned bounding boxes and treats boundary
contact as collision. Each active-link capsule is the declared collision
geometry or a closed outer enclosure of its link mesh. A certificate applies to
one obstacle revision $\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}$: the contract treats each edit as
a new revision and requires all box and edge labels to be invalidated before
the next query; concurrent scene mutation is excluded. Self-collision, attached bodies (including payloads),
grippers, task regions, and other constraints lie outside this model and require
an additional conservative validator and corresponding cache-identity fields.
The experiments include only active rounded-link--workspace-obstacle collisions.
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[Returned-path certificate status]
\label{def:path_witness}
Fix a scene revision $\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}$, its obstacle set
$\calO=\calO(\nu_{\mathrm{scene}})$, the active-link model, and a validation
descriptor $\delta$. We distinguish its endpoint-evidence fields, which record
the endpoint source and split policy, from its current-validation fields, which
record the link representation, active-link set and radii, and numerical policy.
A construction witness for a continuous returned path
$\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Q$ consists of a finite partition
$0=t_0<\cdots<t_m=1$ and regions $R_1,\ldots,R_m$ satisfying
$\gamma([t_{j-1},t_j])\subseteq R_j$ for every $j$. Each $R_j$ is either
(i) an axis-aligned box whose descriptor-matched link envelopes pass the
current-scene conservative separation test, (ii) a zero-gap exact finite union
of such boxes under the same revision and descriptor, or (iii) a
\cspace{} OBB whose complete region passes the conservative affine-zonotope
endpoint/link validator under the same recorded numerical policy.
The reporting contract assigns certificate-backed status to $\gamma$ if and
only if the implementation retains such a finite construction witness for the
entire returned path. The current implementation serializes neither complete
route nor exact-union provenance; all reported \rbf{} returns are therefore
validation-required.
\end{definition}

Certificate-backed status is construction-based and distinct from discrete
audit; postprocessing must retain the complete witness in
\Cref{def:path_witness}.

\subsection{Geometric Foundation}\label{sec:rewrite_geom}

Let \(C_0\) be a body-frame shape. The rigid transform
$T(q)x=R(q)x+t(q)$ maps a body-frame point \(x\) to workspace at configuration
$q$, with rotation $R(q)$ and translation $t(q)$, and
$\mathcal{S}_I(C_0)$ denotes the workspace set swept by \(C_0\) as $q$ ranges
over $I$. Let \(\Ball_p(r)\) denote the radius-\(r\) ball in the \(p\)-norm, and
let \(\oplus\) denote Minkowski sum. For a capsule $C=S\oplus \Ball_2(r)$ with
zero-radius center segment \(S\), the fixed-radius ball commutes with rigid
motion:
\[
\mathcal{S}_I(C)=\mathcal{S}_I(S)\oplus \Ball_2(r).
\]
Thus a rounded link can be bounded by first bounding the zero-radius center
segment and then lifting by the link radius. For a convex body
$P=\operatorname{conv}(v_1,\ldots,v_m)$ with vertices \(v_i\), outer bounds on
the vertex trajectories also bound the swept body,
\[
\mathcal{S}_I(P)\subseteq
\operatorname{conv}\Bigl(\bigcup_{i=1}^{m} \widehat{\Gamma}_i(I)\Bigr).
\]
Here \(\widehat{\Gamma}_i(I)\) conservatively bounds the workspace trajectory of
vertex \(v_i\), \(i=1,\ldots,m\), over \(I\). A capsule link needs only its two
endpoint trajectories. Let
\(\Gamma_a(I)\) and \(\Gamma_b(I)\) denote the exact workspace trajectories of
the proximal and distal endpoints over \(I\). \rbf{} uses these swept-sphere
bounds over a joint-space box and stores the result as
reusable kinematics-keyed bound records.

\begin{samepage}
\begin{proposition}[Endpoint envelopes induce link interval envelopes]
\label{prop:endpoint_to_link_envelope}
Consider a rounded link with body-frame endpoints $a,b$,
$L=[a,b]\oplus \Ball_2(r)$, and any joint region $R\subseteq\Q$. If endpoint
envelopes $E_a(R)$ and $E_b(R)$ satisfy
$\Gamma_a(R)\subseteq E_a(R)$ and $\Gamma_b(R)\subseteq E_b(R)$, then
\[
\mathcal{S}_R(L)
\subseteq
\operatorname{conv}\bigl(E_a(R)\cup E_b(R)\bigr)\oplus \Ball_2(r).
\]
Consequently, any conservative outer representation of the right-hand side is a
valid link envelope for the whole joint region $R$.
\end{proposition}
\end{samepage}

\begin{proof}
For each $q\in R$, the moved center segment is the convex hull of its endpoints.
Hence the union of these segments lies in
\(\operatorname{conv}(\Gamma_a(R)\cup\Gamma_b(R))\subseteq
\operatorname{conv}(E_a(R)\cup E_b(R))\). Adding the fixed-radius ball then
yields the capsule sweep and the stated containment.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Conservative configuration-space box validation]
\label{cor:box_validation}
Let $I\subseteq\Q$ be an axis-aligned configuration-space box and let
$\{B_\ell(I)\}$ be conservative link interval envelopes for all active rounded links. If
$B_\ell(I)\cap\calO=\emptyset$ for every link $\ell$, then every configuration
$q\in I$ is collision-free with respect to the collision model in
\Cref{ass:collision_model}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By conservatism, each $B_\ell(I)$ contains the complete workspace sweep of link
$\ell$ over $I$. Its disjointness from $\calO$ therefore excludes the modeled
workspace-obstacle collision for every active link and every $q\in I$ under
\Cref{ass:collision_model}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Numerical conservatism}
Software soundness requires outward containment, positive descriptor-bound
margins, and failure-closed separation; an inconclusive evaluation cannot
accept a free region. The axis-aligned bounding-box (AABB) broadphase uses
$\Ball_\infty(r_\ell)\supseteq\Ball_2(r_\ell)$, while the support-mapping
representation (SupportHull) with GJK requires outward margins and
failure-closed separation. Because intermediate
floating-point operations are not machine-checked, certificate status is
conditional on this recorded numerical contract.

\subsection{Endpoint Interval Envelopes}\label{sec:rewrite_endpoint_env}

For endpoint $k$, let $\Gamma_k(I)$ be its exact trajectory over joint interval
$I$.
Any conservative set containing $\Gamma_k(I)$ is an endpoint interval envelope;
the basic record is an endpoint axis-aligned bounding box (AABB) because it is
compact and directly reusable by all link-envelope representations; the
implementation calls this record an interval AABB (iAABB). We evaluate two
conservative interval variants. Affine-arithmetic interval forward kinematics
(IFK-AA) performs an unsplit interval pass along the Denavit--Hartenberg chain,
with shared noise symbols preserving part of the trigonometric correlation.
Hierarchical interval forward kinematics (HIFK) bisects the joint interval and
hulls child endpoint boxes, reducing wrapping at higher materialization cost;
its split depth is part of the cache descriptor. Critical-sample probes selected
boundary and trigonometric candidates, while Analytical performs structured
root search for low-order sinusoidal extrema. The reported versions lack an
interval-closure proof and therefore serve as filters. The upper panel of
Fig.~\ref{fig:linkiaabb} summarizes the endpoint-to-link conversion.

\subsection{C-space OBB Endpoint Envelopes}\label{sec:obb_endpoint_env}

Axis-aligned boxes remain the global forest primitive; an OBB is local edge
evidence for a bridge or repair. Let \(q_c\) be its center, let diagonal
\(S\succ0\) scale the joints, let \(U\in\Real^{n\times m}\) have orthonormal
columns in scaled coordinates, and let \(h\in\Real_{\ge0}^{m}\) contain the
half-widths. With \(A=S^{-1}U\operatorname{diag}(h)\) and
\(\Xi=\{\xi\in\Real^m:-1\le\xi_i\le1\}\), the joint-space region is
\[
R_{\mathrm{obb}}
=
\{\,q_c + A\xi \mid \xi\in\Xi\,\}.
\]
Certificate eligibility requires \(R_{\mathrm{obb}}\subseteq\Q\), verified
containment of the represented path window, and conservative validation of the
complete region; the fitted orientation alone provides no safety status.

\paragraph{Finite path cover}
For a piecewise-linear candidate path \(\gamma\), recursively selected windows
\(J_\tau\subseteq[0,1]\) and OBBs \(R_\tau\) must establish
\(\gamma(J_\tau)\subseteq R_\tau\subseteq\Q\). A successful cover satisfies
\[
[0,1]=\bigcup_{\tau=1}^{M}J_\tau,\qquad
\gamma([0,1])\subseteq\bigcup_{\tau=1}^{M}R_\tau
\subseteq\Cfree(\calO).
\]
Every complete OBB region must pass the correlated endpoint/link test below.
An uncovered window, exhausted budget, or inconclusive test rejects the cover;
sampled fallbacks remain search-only.

\begin{corollary}[Finite-cover OBB-path soundness]
\label{cor:obb_corridor}
Fix a scene revision $\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}$ and descriptor $\delta$, and write
$\calO=\calO(\nu_{\mathrm{scene}})$. Let
$[0,1]=\bigcup_{\tau=1}^{M}J_\tau$ be a finite window cover of a continuous
path $\gamma$, with $\gamma(J_\tau)\subseteq R_\tau\subseteq\Q$. Suppose that,
for every $\tau$, conservative affine-zonotope endpoint enclosures induce
conservative envelopes for all active links over the complete region $R_\tau$,
and that those link envelopes pass current-scene separation under the same
revision and descriptor. Then $\gamma([0,1])\subseteq\Cfree(\calO)$ under
\Cref{ass:collision_model}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For each $\tau$, \Cref{prop:endpoint_to_link_envelope} and current-scene
separation give $R_\tau\subseteq\Cfree(\calO)$. For any $t\in[0,1]$, the window
cover supplies an index $\tau$ with $t\in J_\tau$, and hence
$\gamma(t)\in R_\tau\subseteq\Cfree(\calO)$. The claim follows.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Correlated endpoint envelope}
For \(q=q_c+A\xi\), outward affine propagation gives
\[
E_k(R_{\mathrm{obb}})
=
c_k+\sum_i g_{k,i}\epsilon_i \oplus [-\rho_k,\rho_k].
\]
Here \(\rho_k\) outwardly encloses all nonlinear remainder; a sampled remainder
is filter-only. For workspace direction \(d\in\mathbb{R}^3\), its support is
\[
h_{E_k}(d)=d^\top c_k+\sum_i |d^\top g_{k,i}|
          +\sum_{\mu=1}^3 |d_\mu|\rho_{k,\mu}.
\]
The rounded-link support bound is therefore
\[
\bar h_\ell(d)=
\max\{h_{E_a}(d),h_{E_b}(d)\}+r_\ell\|d\|_2.
\]
Coordinate directions provide the AABB broadphase; retained supports provide
the SupportHull/GJK test. OBB records remain scene-local typed-edge evidence
and do not become persistent LECT records or global forest nodes.

\subsection{Link-Envelope Representations}\label{sec:rewrite_link_env}

Endpoint records can be exported to multiple link representations without
changing the upstream kinematic source. For a serial-chain capsule link \(\ell\)
whose proximal and distal endpoint records are \(E_{\ell-1}(I)\) and
\(E_\ell(I)\),
$B_\ell(I)=B_\ell^\circ(I)\oplus\mathcal{R}(r_\ell)$, with
$B_\ell^\circ(I)$ bounding the centerline sweep and \(\mathcal{R}(r_\ell)\)
denoting the selected radius-lift set for link radius \(r_\ell\).

\paragraph{AABB envelope}
AABB envelopes wrap the two endpoint envelopes in one box and pad by the link
radius:
\[
B_\ell(I) = \operatorname{bbox}\bigl(E_{\ell-1}(I) \cup E_\ell(I)\bigr)
\oplus \Ball_\infty(r_\ell).
\]
Here \(\operatorname{bbox}(\cdot)\) denotes the workspace AABB hull.
Segment subdivision may retain an explicit union of padded sub-envelopes for
more selective filtering; collapsing that union back to one box would lose the
tighter bound.

\paragraph{Fixed-direction slab envelope}
A \(k\)-direction discrete oriented polytope (\(k\)-DOP) stores radius-padded
endpoint support intervals along fixed directions. After the AABB broadphase,
these slabs can reject candidates before GJK; only conservative separation can
contribute to a $\mathrm{CertFree}$ decision.

\paragraph{SupportHull envelope}
SupportHull stores support mappings for both endpoint enclosures and the link
radius. The default implementation uses endpoint AABBs; correlated OBB
corridors use the zonotope-plus-box records above. A bounded-work GJK narrow
phase uses outward obstacle inflation, and an unresolved iteration limit returns
$\mathrm{Unknown}$ rather than a free label.

\section{Lifelong Envelope Cache Tree}\label{sec:lect}

LECT persists versioned, obstacle-independent endpoint evidence across
compatible builds. \rbf{} derives link envelopes under the current descriptor
and refreshes every scene-dependent label; graph connectivity and query
outcomes never cross scene revisions.

\subsection{Cache Key and Split Policy}\label{sec:lect_design}

LECT is a dynamically grown binary \(k\)-d tree over $\Q$; each node stores a
joint interval, split metadata, and geometry records.

\paragraph{Cache identity}
Replay requires a fixed base frame and an exact joint-region and endpoint-
evidence identity match. The endpoint key binds robot kinematics, joint limits, tool
frame, endpoint map, root domain, canonicalization and symmetry modes, split
policy, endpoint descriptor, payload layout, schema, and builder version. Link
radii are excluded; active links, radii, link representation, and numerical
policy remain current-validation fields of $\delta$. For symmetry sector $s$,
$\phi_s$ maps a native interval to its canonical interval and $\Psi_s$ maps the
cached enclosure back. Replay is allowed only if $\Psi_s$ preserves outward
containment; otherwise evidence is recomputed in native coordinates. The
evaluated persistent store contains endpoint records only. Derived-link
envelopes may be memoized within the current oracle, but are not replayed across
revisions and carry no scene label.

Direct database replay checks the full semantic identity. The evaluated
memory-mapped snapshot is assumed to be the immutable, co-generated image of its verified
companion store; its header alone does not establish full semantic identity.

At schedule depth $h$, let $\mathcal{S}_h$ be a bounded deterministic sample of
intervals and $I_d^{-},I_d^{+}$ the midpoint children in dimension $d$. The
depth-synchronous policy selects
\[
d_h^*=\operatorname*{lex\,argmin}_d
\left(u_d,
\sum_{I\in\mathcal{S}_h}\max_{\eta\in\{-,+\}}\mu(I_d^\eta),d\right),
\]
where $d$ ranges over dimensions splittable for at least one
$I\in\mathcal{S}_h$, $u_d$ counts unsplittable samples, and $\mu$ is the
descriptor-specific child score; an unsplittable child contributes its parent
score. For IFK-AA,
$\mu$ sums active-endpoint iAABB volumes; SupportHull uses its corresponding
volume score. The tuple favors broadly splittable dimensions, then the smaller
sum of per-sample worst-child scores, with $d$ breaking ties. Ordered expansion
and sampling make the schedule deterministic for a fixed root and descriptor;
a step is defined only while the candidate set is nonempty. For a runtime
interval unsplittable along $d_h^*$ under the split policy, resolution uses the
widest strictly positive-width allowed dimension, breaking equal-width ties by
the smaller dimension index; if none exists, the interval is terminal.

\paragraph{Sparse interval staging}
Planning may stage an exact interval without materializing every ancestor. If
its endpoint record is absent, that interval is materialized before validation
under the current descriptor.

\subsection{Runtime Use and Validation Semantics}

For a joint interval, LECT replays or materializes endpoint evidence, derives
link envelopes under $\delta$, and applies the current-scene validator. A
complete child partition may form a parent hull, but the parent still passes
the same validator. Missing or inconclusive evidence yields
$\mathrm{Unknown}$. Cache hits may change enclosure tightness and hence the
acceptance outcome, but not the meaning or validation obligations of any
returned status.

\begin{proposition}[Current-scene safety from conservative replayed endpoint evidence]
\label{prop:scene_safe_replay}
Let $I\subseteq\Q$ be an axis-aligned LECT joint box, and fix the
base-to-workspace frame, current scene revision $\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}$, and
descriptor $\delta$. Assume that cached endpoint records
conservatively enclose every active-link endpoint trajectory over $I$, that
decoding---including any canonical-to-native mapping---preserves outward
containment, and that the joint-region key and all recorded endpoint-evidence
identity fields match. If link envelopes derived under the current-validation
fields of $\delta$ are conservative and all pass separation from
$\calO(\nu_{\mathrm{scene}})$, then
$I\subseteq\Cfree(\calO(\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}))$ under
\Cref{ass:collision_model}. This conclusion uses no label from an earlier
scene. Replayed and freshly materialized records may differ and hence need not
yield identical acceptance decisions.
\end{proposition}

Identity matching establishes replay eligibility, not containment; conservative
cached evidence remains an independent premise.

\begin{proof}
The replay premises preserve all endpoint inclusions.
\Cref{prop:endpoint_to_link_envelope} therefore places each rounded-link sweep
inside its derived conservative envelope. Current-scene separation and
\Cref{cor:box_validation} give the stated inclusion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Storage and Revision Semantics}\label{sec:lect_storage}

Persistent units are endpoint records; link envelopes are derived under the
current descriptor and revalidated against the scene. Prewarming fills leaf
records and derives ancestor hulls from complete child evidence, but imports no
box or edge label. A scene revision invalidates all prior labels. Insertions and
deletions may select boxes and edges for rechecking, but every retained or
promoted object is relabeled only after current-scene validation; compatible
LECT geometry remains reusable.

\section{RapidBoxForest Construction}\label{sec:forest}

Fig.~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes the planning layer. \rbf{} turns envelope
validation and LECT replay into a query-agnostic scene structure of
scene-specific, policy-accepted \cspace{} boxes and typed corridor adjacency.
For a fixed obstacle set, later queries use box membership and graph search;
LECT remains a separate evidence store. Under the construction contract, every
accepted box is either checked against the current obstacles or inherits status
through a same-revision, descriptor-matched exact-union witness.

A box is an axis-aligned joint interval
$B=[l^{(1)},u^{(1)}]\times\cdots\times[l^{(n)},u^{(n)}]\subseteq\Q$.
A \emph{policy-accepted box} has passed the configured scene-validation policy.
Under the stated geometric and numerical assumptions, conservative link-envelope
disjointness makes it eligible for \(\mathrm{CertFree}\); filter-only acceptance
yields a validation-required planning region. Rejected cells are excluded from
the forest, while selected rejected or unresolved domains are
retained for local refinement or later update.

\subsection{Budgeted Construction Contract}\label{sec:forest_objective}

Construction returns a budget-limited reusable cover rather than solving a
maximum-volume, minimum-cardinality, or exhaustive-decomposition problem.
Given the joint-limit box $\Q$ and obstacle set $\calO$, the scene policy
$V_{\calO}(B;\delta)$ evaluates active-link envelopes for $B$ under descriptor
$\delta$ and has range
\[
V_{\calO}(B;\delta)\in\{\mathrm{CertFree},\mathrm{ProvisionalFree},
\mathrm{Occupied},\mathrm{Unknown}\}.
\]
The general accepted set admits the first two states; the evaluated
strict-certificate build commits only \(\mathrm{CertFree}\) cells.
This status requires every active-link set \(B_\ell(B;\delta)\) to be a
conservative outer envelope disjoint from \(\calO\) under the recorded
failure-closed numerical policy. \(\mathrm{ProvisionalFree}\) denotes
filter-only acceptance and remains validation-required;
\(\mathrm{Occupied}\) is a policy rejection, not a claim that all of \(B\) is
in the configuration-space obstacle region; and \(\mathrm{Unknown}\) denotes
inconclusive or resource-limited evaluation. Rejected and unknown domains may
be retained for refinement or update but never gain certificate status from a
probe or discrete audit. The accepted region set of \(N\) boxes is
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal{F}=\bigl\{(B_i,\sigma_i):i=1,\ldots,N\bigr\},\\
B_i\subseteq\Q,\qquad \sigma_i\in
\{\mathrm{CertFree},\mathrm{ProvisionalFree}\}.
\end{gathered}
\]
For a directly validated box, \(\sigma_i=V_{\calO}(B_i;\delta)\). For a
finite group \(\mathcal{G}\) of \(\mathrm{CertFree}\) boxes validated under
one scene revision and descriptor, let
\(H=\operatorname{bbox}(\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{G}}B)\). The hull inherits
\(\mathrm{CertFree}\) only when the zero-gap set equality
\[
\operatorname{ExactUnion}(H,\mathcal{G})
\Longleftrightarrow H=\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{G}}B
\]
holds. Connection tolerance \(\varepsilon_c\) is not used in this predicate;
a tolerance-gap or nonexact hull requires direct whole-hull validation and is
otherwise validation-required.
In the reported runs, certificate inheritance is restricted to
partition-native contiguous grid ranges, whose integer cell ranges establish
the set equality; Q3 reports no coalescing. Tolerance-based merger paths are
outside this certificate contract.
The reusable output is $(\mathcal{F},G)$, where \(G\) is the typed corridor
graph of adjacencies and auxiliary transitions. The box budget targets fixed,
query-independent anchors, frontiers, and connectors. ``Forest'' denotes rooted
box-growth components before consolidation; the returned graph may contain
cycles, and merged records need not retain ancestry.

For two boxes
\[
B_i=\prod_{d=1}^{n}[l_i^{(d)},u_i^{(d)}],\qquad
B_j=\prod_{d=1}^{n}[l_j^{(d)},u_j^{(d)}],
\]
let \(\varepsilon_c\ge 0\) denote the connection tolerance. \rbf{} treats the
boxes as candidate-adjacent when the closed intervals in every coordinate
overlap or have a gap no larger than \(\varepsilon_c\):
\[
\max\{l_i^{(d)},l_j^{(d)}\}
\le
\min\{u_i^{(d)},u_j^{(d)}\}+\varepsilon_c,\qquad d=1,\ldots,n.
\]
Only exact overlap or contact directly yields a contained box-corridor.
Tolerance-gap and explicit segment edges are graph-search aids, not
certificates, unless they are converted into conservative box-corridor or
finite-cover OBB corridor regions; otherwise they require fixed-step validation.

\subsection{Build Pipeline}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:leaf_sweep_refine} summarizes construction and certificate
inheritance. It validates a descriptor-aligned frontier in batches, commits
only $\mathrm{CertFree}$ cells, and coalesces only zero-gap exact unions of
certificate-only inputs. Remaining maximal frontier cells enter an
anchor-prioritized queue keyed by exact region identity.

Let $\widehat V_{\calO}(I;\delta)$ obtain endpoint evidence, derive current link
envelopes, and apply $V_{\calO}$. Any unrecovered replay, materialization,
decoding, envelope-construction, or validation failure returns
$\mathrm{Unknown}$. For a seed $q$, the logical failure-closed descent follows
the deterministic root-to-$q$ chain through
\[
D_{\mathrm{eff}}
=\max\{0,\min(D_{\max},D_{\mathrm{tree}}(q)-1)\}
\]
where $D_{\max}$ is the requested depth cap and $D_{\mathrm{tree}}(q)$ is the
number of available or constructible levels. A linear reference descent returns
the first policy-accepted cell; at the cap or a terminal cell, it retains the
current domain and status. The evaluated helper instead probes a fixed sparse
subset of depths, validating every probed cell independently; it need not find
the shallowest or every existing accepted depth. No failed evaluation creates
an accepted label. Query-specific endpoint attachments and online corridors
remain outside the reusable build and follow \Cref{tab:claim_boundary}.

\paragraph{Finite work}
The reference chain visits at most $D_{\mathrm{eff}}+1$ cells and requires
$O(n(D_{\mathrm{eff}}+1))$ bookkeeping with dimension scans. The evaluated
sparse-depth helper probes $O(1+\log(D_{\mathrm{eff}}+1))$ depths. Let $r$ be
the number of root sectors, $0\le D_0\le D_s$ the shallow-sweep depths, and
$B_{\max}$ the adaptive-validation cap. An unpruned sweep from $D_0$ through $D_s$
validates at most $r(2^{D_s+1}-2^{D_0})$ cells, and adaptive refinement adds at
most $B_{\max}$ unique-cell validations. Exact pairwise overlap construction is $O(nN^2)$
for $N$ boxes in $n$ dimensions; replay, envelope construction, and separation
remain oracle costs. Finite budgets and bounded OBB-cover loops guarantee
termination, but not coverage or completeness.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Zero-gap exact coalescing and budgeted refinement.}
\label{alg:leaf_sweep_refine}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Anchors $\mathcal{S}$, LECT $\mathcal{T}_L$, obstacles $\calO$,
policy $V_{\calO}$, descriptor $\delta$, depths $0\le D_0\le D_s$, validation cap
$B_{\max}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge1}$, and resource caps
\ENSURE Labeled boxes $\mathcal{F}$, overlap subgraph $G_\cap$, retained domains $\mathcal{C}$
\STATE batch-evaluate the identity-deduplicated frontier through depths
$D_0{:}D_s$: $s_I\leftarrow\widehat V_{\calO}(I;\delta)$
\STATE exact-coalesce same-revision CertFree groups satisfying
$\operatorname{ExactUnion}$
\STATE insert merged $(B,\mathrm{CertFree})$ and unmerged
$(I,\mathrm{CertFree})$ records into $\mathcal{F}$; build $G_\cap$
\STATE initialize $\mathcal{P}$ with maximal remaining frontier cells, keyed by
region identity and prioritized by anchor coverage;
$\mathcal{C}\leftarrow\emptyset$; $b\leftarrow0$
\WHILE{$\mathcal{P}$ is nonempty, $b<B_{\max}$, and time/depth caps remain}
  \STATE admit a batch of at most $B_{\max}-b$ cells;
  $b\leftarrow b+|\mathrm{batch}|$
  \STATE evaluate $s_I\leftarrow\widehat V_{\calO}(I;\delta)$ for each admitted cell
  \FORALL{cells $I$ in the batch}
    \IF{$s_I=\mathrm{CertFree}$}
      \STATE insert $(I,\mathrm{CertFree})$ into $\mathcal{F}$ and update $G_\cap$
    \ELSIF{$I$ is splittable within the caps}
      \STATE enqueue unseen descriptor-aligned children of $I$
    \ELSE
      \STATE defer $I$ into $\mathcal{C}$
    \ENDIF
  \ENDFOR
\ENDWHILE
\STATE retain the capacity-limited union of $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{P}$;
record omissions
\RETURN $\mathcal{F},G_\cap,\mathcal{C}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Here $G_\cap=(\mathcal{F},E_\cap)$ is the reusable overlap subgraph; the corridor
stage adds the remaining typed edge sets to form $G$.

\subsection{Corridor Query and Certificate}
\label{sec:forest_graph}

The typed graph separates search adjacency from certifying corridors:
$G=(\mathcal{F},E_{\cap}\cup E_{\varepsilon}\cup E_s\cup
E_{\mathrm{obb}}\cup E_{\mathrm{rep}})$, comprising overlap/contact, tolerance
gap, segment, finite-cover OBB-corridor, and repair edges. Overlap/contact and
conservatively validated OBB corridors can support certificate-backed status;
other edges do so only after conversion to a conservative corridor. Eligibility
is determined by the retained witness, not the edge label. Fix the active scene
revision $\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}$ and descriptor $\delta$. Let route provenance be
\(W=\bigl\langle(t_{j-1},t_j,R_j)\bigr\rangle_{j=1}^{m}\), where
\(0=t_0<\cdots<t_m=1\) and \(R_j\) is the witness region for
\(\gamma([t_{j-1},t_j])\). Define certificate-backed status by
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cert}_{\nu_{\mathrm{scene}},\delta}(\gamma,W)
\Longleftrightarrow{}&
\operatorname{Contain}(\gamma,W)\\[-1mm]
&{}\land\!\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}
\operatorname{SceneValid}_{\mathrm{cons}}
(R_j;\nu_{\mathrm{scene}},\delta).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $\operatorname{Contain}$ is the finite subpath-containment condition in
\Cref{def:path_witness}. The predicate
$\operatorname{SceneValid}_{\mathrm{cons}}
(R;\nu_{\mathrm{scene}},\delta)$ denotes direct conservative validation under
that scene revision and descriptor, or validity
inherited by a zero-gap \(\operatorname{ExactUnion}\) witness. Every
box, OBB region, query-endpoint attachment, and transition contributing to the
complete path must satisfy \Cref{def:path_witness}; one unsupported component
downgrades the full return.

Endpoints attach only through containing cells. A best-first traversal uses a
heuristic priority and makes no shortest-path claim. If $N_{\mathrm{adj}}$ adjacency entries
are examined, $N_{\mathrm{rel}}$ relaxations succeed, the heap contains at most
$H$ entries, and each adjacency incurs at most $T_e$ query-dependent work, the
search cost is
\[
O\!\left(N_{\mathrm{adj}}(1+T_e)+(N_{\mathrm{rel}}+1)\log(H+1)\right).
\]
For overlap routes, gateways lie in consecutive box intersections, and
convexity keeps their connecting segments within witnessed boxes.

\begin{lemma}[Box-corridor certificate]
\label{lem:corridor_certificate}
Fix a scene revision $\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}$ and descriptor $\delta$. Let
$\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cert}}:=\{B_i\mid
\sigma_i=\mathrm{CertFree}\}$ contain forest boxes labeled under $\delta$
against $\calO(\nu_{\mathrm{scene}})$, each either validated directly by
conservative link envelopes or formed through a zero-gap exact-union witness.
Define
\(R_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cert}}}:=
\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cert}}}B\).
If a continuous path $\gamma:[0,1]\to\Q$ satisfies
\(\gamma([0,1])\subseteq R_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cert}}}\), then
\[
\gamma([0,1])\subseteq\Cfree(\calO(\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}))
\]
under \Cref{ass:collision_model}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For any $s\in[0,1]$, containment gives
$B\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cert}}$ with $\gamma(s)\in B$. If $B$ passed the
conservative test directly, \Cref{cor:box_validation} applies. If it is an exact
coalesced union, $\gamma(s)$ lies in at least one conservatively validated input
box under the same revision and descriptor, so the same corollary applies there.
Because $s$ was arbitrary, the stated path inclusion follows.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Q3: Random Multi-Robot Scenes}
\label{sec:exp_generalization}

Q3 characterizes audited paths and path quality for six catalog-tuned
Medium/Hard profiles across three robots.

\emph{Dataset and query construction.}
The frozen catalog contains three robots, ten seeds per difficulty, and ten
queries per scene. Q3 evaluates all 60 Medium/Hard scenes (600 evaluations): 300
start--goal pairs shared across paired obstacle prefixes, with Hard strictly
extending Medium. Every direct start--goal segment is obstructed. A fixed
planner-probe policy conditioned catalog admission on obstruction and
feasibility; every method was then rerun on the complete admitted set.

\emph{Compared methods and accounting.}
\rbf{} reuses one query-agnostic forest across each scene's ten saved-order
queries. The six profiles were selected on this catalog for 100/100 audit
eligibility using Task/q and path length. The Q3 PRM/BIT* baselines retain Q2's
structural profiles with manifested horizon overrides. PRM shares one
endpoint-preloaded roadmap per scene. RRT-Connect and BIT* run independently per query; BIT*
selects the smallest audit-passing solve coordinate within 8\% of each saved
query's best path. For PRM, curve abscissae use configured checkpoints, whereas
the table orders 100/100 points by measured Build/5+query and advances only after
each $\geq1\%$ aggregate improvement. BIT* curve rings also track successive
$\geq1\%$ aggregate improvements, whereas table entries use the query-wise 8\%
rule above.
IRIS/GCS is omitted because no common-protocol Q3 result was available.

\emph{Results.}
At the six eligible points, \rbf{} medians are 0.02--0.31 below RRT-Connect and
lower than PRM in five conditions. They are 0.01--0.05 below BIT* in five
conditions and 0.01 higher for Panda-Medium. Mean \rbf{} Build/$K$+Task/q falls
70--79\% from $K=1$ to $K=10$.

\subsection{Q4: Endpoint and Link-Envelope Mechanisms}
\label{sec:exp_mechanisms}

Q4 reports six-width joint-interval sweeps in
Tables~\ref{tab:tro-endpoint-envelope} and~\ref{tab:tro-link-envelope}. Each
study uses the same deterministic set of 1000 IIWA joint boxes per width for all
representations. The endpoint study compares construction time and volume. Its
Gap metric is the negative of the largest per-axis extent-width shortfall
relative to the union of Critical-sample, Analytical, and Monte Carlo extents;
it is a span diagnostic. Endpoint entries are medians and worst-case Gap,
whereas link entries are means.

The link study isolates representation cost, not the production AABB-first
cascade. For each box and representation, Test pools five timing repeats in two
cases: the Shelf obstacle set translated 20~m along the \(x\)-axis, and a
method-specific 10-mm known-overlap AABB centered in that representation's first
link envelope. Both cases scan all link--obstacle pairs without early exit.
Link AABB uses overlap tests; SupportHull invokes GJK with its AABB broadphase
disabled. Thus the measurements quantify envelope-build and narrow-phase test
costs and motivate the production choice of cheap AABB rejection before
optional SupportHull/GJK refinement.

\noindent\emph{Takeaway.}
At 0.50~rad, HIFK-5 has normalized endpoint volume
2.07 versus 8.61 for IFK-AA (76\% lower) at 9.8 times the endpoint-construction
cost. Across the tested widths, isolated AABB overlap takes 4.7--6.1\% of the
mean per-probe time required by direct SupportHull/GJK\@. Q4 isolates the
mechanisms whose system-level interaction appears in Q1.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

\rbf{} combines two-level volumetric reuse with a failure-closed route-status
contract. Within a scene, it reuses a budgeted graph of joint boxes with
zero-gap exact-union coalescing. Across compatible revisions, direct-store LECT
replay admits only identity-matched endpoint evidence; the evaluated snapshot
assumes an immutable, co-generated image of its verified companion store. Link
envelopes are re-derived, and scene labels and connectivity must be rebuilt
under the current descriptor. Retained typed witnesses separate search from
certificate-backed connectivity.

On Shelf+IIWA, warm IFK-AA replay and on-demand HIFK-5 materialization yield
median builds of 0.171~s and 2.770~s, respectively---a 16.2-fold
compound-profile difference. Across six in-catalog Q3 conditions with
method-specific operating-point selection, \rbf{}'s query-matched median path
ratios span 1.00--1.05, versus 1.02--1.36 for RRT-Connect and 1.04--1.07 for
BIT*. Amortizing Build over ten queries lowers \rbf{}'s Medium/Hard mean
build-plus-task diagnostic by 70--79\% relative to one query. At 0.50~rad,
HIFK-5 reduces normalized endpoint volume by 76\% at 9.8
times the IFK-AA construction cost; isolated AABB tests take 4.7--6.1\% of
direct SupportHull/GJK probe time.

\noindent\emph{Limitations.}
Evaluation is software-only and uses condition-tuned points without held-out Q3
selection. The Q1/Q3 artifacts lack complete final-route provenance, and the
experiments do not test online revision or cache mismatch. The collision model
covers active rounded links against AABB obstacles. Finite depth and
bridge/repair budgets forfeit completeness, the 12.74-GiB complete d23 tree
scales exponentially, and certificate status remains conditional on the stated
floating-point contract.

\rbf{} therefore provides an auditable volumetric reuse layer that
complements roadmap reuse and convex-region optimization.
