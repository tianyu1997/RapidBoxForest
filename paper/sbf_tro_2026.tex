\title{RapidBoxForest: Cross-Revision Endpoint-Envelope Reuse for Region-Graph Motion Planning}

\author{Anonymous Authors}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Repeated-query motion planning in changing workcells requires geometric reuse
without carrying obsolete collision claims across revisions.
RapidBoxForest (\rbf{}) stores obstacle-independent endpoint envelopes in a
Lifelong Envelope Cache Tree (LECT), keyed by canonical dyadic addresses
shared with region-graph primitives. Across compatible revisions,
exact-key replay avoids rematching, while link envelopes, obstacle labels, and
adjacency are rebuilt for the current scene. A budgeted builder refines
cells, coalesces exact dyadic unions, and constructs corridors from current-scene
witnesses. Under stated collision and conservative-validation assumptions,
a finite cover of validated regions guarantees collision freedom
throughout a certificate-backed continuous path, without sampled collision checks.
The Shelf+IIWA warm profile completed all 40 tasks; median reusable-build time
and within-batch completion timestamp were 0.171 and 0.077~s.
Holding per-task time fixed,
the ten-query build-plus-task projection was 69.6--79.7\% below the one-query
projection across three robots; this arithmetic result is not a measured
multi-query or cross-revision speedup. At method-specific points selected on
this six-condition catalog, each \rbf{} profile completed its 100 instances.
The median empirical-reference-normalized
path-length ratios for \rbf{} were numerically below the corresponding
RRT-Connect medians in all six conditions and below the corresponding medians
for probabilistic roadmaps (PRM) and Batch Informed Trees (BIT*) in five.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Motion planning, robot manipulators, multi-query planning, collision checking,
configuration-space decomposition, link envelopes.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Manipulators often solve sequences of related queries in semi-static workcells,
where robot kinematics remain fixed but start--goal pairs and local clutter
change. A reusable planner should amortize free-space construction across
queries, isolate obstacle-independent kinematic evidence from revision-local
validity, and retain conservative current-scene support for every path segment
claimed to be collision-free.

Planning methods support different forms of reuse. Probabilistic roadmaps,
experience graphs, and safety certificates reuse connectivity, prior paths, or
clearance neighborhoods, whereas standard tree and batch planners construct
query-local search structures~\cite{kavraki1996probabilistic,
bialkowski2016safety,coleman2015experience}. Region-based methods---including
certified decompositions, safe-box planners, and motion-planning Graphs of
Convex Sets (GCS)---operate on scene-specific configuration-space sets and
graphs~\cite{dai2024certified,marcucci2024fastboxes,
marcucci2024shortest,morozov2026multiquery}. This separation raises a central
question: can a region graph be reconstructed for each scene revision while
obstacle-independent kinematic evidence is reused through exact identity
matching? Table~\ref{tab:related_qualitative_comparison} positions the proposed
RapidBoxForest (\rbf{}) framework and its Lifelong Envelope Cache Tree (LECT)
relative to representative planning families.

Each LECT record and its corresponding primitive forest cell share a canonical
dyadic address. Replay therefore uses exact-key lookup rather than geometric
rematching, while validation and refinement operate on the same address space.
Only obstacle-independent endpoint records persist across compatible revisions;
\rbf{} reconstructs link envelopes, box labels, and adjacency for the current
scene. Coalesced scene nodes represent exact dyadic unions but do not serve as
persistent cache keys.

\paragraph{Problem statement}
Let $\Q\subset\Real^n$ be the joint-limit box of a fixed
$n$-degree-of-freedom manipulator, and let $\{\calO^{(r)}\}$ denote a sequence
of revisions to the workspace obstacles. Endpoint-record identity $\kappa_E$ specifies
the robot endpoint model and canonical dyadic decomposition, whereas descriptor
$\delta$ specifies the active-link geometry and validator settings. For fixed
$\delta$, $\Cfree(\calO^{(r)})\subseteq\Q$ denotes the modeled free
configuration space; its dependence on $\delta$ is suppressed below. Each query
provides $(q_s,q_g)\in\Q^2$, and \rbf{} constructs a finite graph of
policy-accepted configuration-space boxes. Queries within one revision share
this graph. Across revisions, an endpoint record is replayed only when both its
joint region and $\kappa_E$ match exactly; link envelopes, box labels, and
connectivity are then recomputed. The objective is a resource-bounded
volumetric planner whose collision-free claims follow solely from conservative
current-scene witnesses.

A scene revision is \emph{compatible} when it preserves $\kappa_E$ and the
canonical address semantics. The descriptor $\delta$ may change, but every
$\delta$-dependent link envelope, label, adjacency, and validation result must
then be reconstructed for the current scene.

\paragraph{Reuse and continuous-path semantics}
\rbf{} separates persistent endpoint geometry from revision-local graph state.
A returned path is certificate-backed only if a complete finite cover of
conservative current-scene witnesses is retained; any unsupported transition
leaves the returned path validation-required.

The paper makes three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{Cross-revision evidence interface.}
  We introduce an exact identity contract that maps every persistent endpoint
  record to one canonical dyadic forest cell. This contract permits reuse of
  obstacle-independent geometry while requiring all scene-dependent state to
  be reconstructed.
  \item \emph{Cache-aligned construction.}
  We develop a resource-bounded builder that replays or materializes endpoint
  records, refines unresolved canonical cells, and coalesces cells only through
  exact dyadic unions with retained provenance, thereby preserving primitive
  cache keys and the certified free set.
  \item \emph{Continuous-path collision freedom.}
  We prove pointwise collision freedom for any returned path covered by finitely
  many conservatively validated axis-aligned boxes or oriented bounding boxes
  (OBBs), under the stated collision and numerical assumptions. Any unsupported
  tolerance-gap, segment, or repair transition leaves the returned path
  validation-required.
\end{enumerate}
Sections~\ref{sec:link_envelopes}--\ref{sec:forest} develop the method
(Fig.~\ref{fig:linkiaabb}); Q1--Q4 evaluate the warm-cache cost profile,
planning trade-offs at the reported operating points, and endpoint- and
link-envelope mechanisms.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Region-based guarantees vary by method. IRIS (Iterative Regional Inflation by
Semidefinite Programming) constructs convex regions, configuration-space IRIS
(C-IRIS) certifies polytopes~\cite{deits2015computing,dai2024certified}, and
graphics-processing-unit (GPU) methods construct probabilistically collision-free
convex sets~\cite{werner2025superfast,werner2026faster}. Visibility-graph clique
covers reduce the number of sets~\cite{werner2024approximating}. Fast safe-box
planning preprocesses a safe-region collection~\cite{marcucci2024fastboxes},
whereas GCS optimizes trajectories over convex-region
graphs~\cite{marcucci2023motion,marcucci2024shortest}. Implicit GCS methods
(IxG/IxG*) and GCS* restrict search to query-relevant portions of an implicit
region graph~\cite{natarajan2024ixg,chewchia2026gcsstar}; multi-query GCS
precomputes a cost-to-go surrogate for repeated queries on a fixed
  GCS~\cite{morozov2026multiquery}. These methods construct or search region
  graphs whose geometric sets and successor rules are scene dependent. \rbf{}
  instead couples construction with revision handling in a dyadic region graph:
  its primitive cells index
obstacle-independent endpoint envelopes, while all obstacle-dependent labels
and overlaps are recomputed after a revision.

\paragraph{Closest methods}
ReUse-based Probabilistic Roadmap (RU-PRM) reuses obstacle-local roadmaps by
matching and transforming corresponding models across similar
environments~\cite{lien2009reuse}; Ball Tree associates volumes with nodes of a
single-query search tree~\cite{shkolnik2011volumes}. Safety certificates cache
local clearance~\cite{bialkowski2016safety}. Dynamic roadmaps, decomposed
collision checking, and Simple Polyhedral Intersection Techniques (SPITE)
reduce revalidation work for pre-existing sampled graphs; SPITE, in particular,
associates configurations and edges with workspace swept-volume approximations
and tests those volumes against moved obstacles~\cite{leven2002framework,
hartmann2023decomposed,ashur2026spite}. Experience graphs retain prior path or
graph experience~\cite{coleman2015experience}, whereas soft subdivision targets
conservative cell classification~\cite{wang2015soft}. These methods differ in
the state retained across revisions. C-IRIS constructs scene-specific certified
regions; GCS optimizes over a supplied scene-specific region graph. RU-PRM,
dynamic roadmaps, and SPITE retain or update sampled graph elements; clearance
caches retain local validity neighborhoods.
Across revisions, \rbf{} retains neither scene-validity labels nor connectivity.
Instead, it reuses obstacle-independent endpoint envelopes indexed by canonical
volumetric cells and reconstructs current-scene labels, overlaps, and corridor
witnesses.

\paragraph{Endpoint bounds}
\label{sec:related_endpoint_bounds}
Common tools for geometric proximity queries include the
Gilbert--Johnson--Keerthi (GJK) algorithm~\cite{gilbert1988fast} and the
  Flexible Collision Library (FCL)~\cite{pan2012fcl}. Interval methods provide
conservative bounds for nonlinear robot kinematics~\cite{moore2009introduction,
merlet2009interval}. Articulated continuous collision detection (CCD) has used
interval hierarchies, swept primitives, and Taylor models to bound
motion~\cite{redon2005fast_artic,zhang2007taylor}. \rbf{} adopts interval
bounding within a cross-revision reuse contract: versioned,
obstacle-independent endpoint envelopes may persist, whereas link envelopes and
obstacle-separation tests are recomputed for the current scene.

\paragraph{Graph reuse}
The probabilistic roadmap (PRM) amortizes sampled-roadmap construction across
queries, whereas LazyPRM
defers sampled-edge validation~\cite{kavraki1996probabilistic,
bohlin2000path}. Effort Informed Roadmaps (EIRM*), decomposed collision
checking, and Repetition Roadmaps reuse prior edges, collision-test results, or
paths~\cite{hartmann2023eirm,hartmann2023decomposed,
lehner2018repetition}. Adaptive cell planners refine graphs of accepted
cells~\cite{lozanoperez1983spatial,brooks1985subdivision,wang2015soft}. \rbf{}
instead assigns canonical volumetric cells two roles: they index reusable,
obstacle-independent endpoint records and form the primitives of a newly
labeled overlap graph constructed under an explicit resource budget.

\paragraph{Sampling and refinement}
RRT-Connect is a bidirectional single-query planner~\cite{kuffner2000rrt}, and
Batch Informed Trees (BIT*) is an informed, anytime, asymptotically optimal sampling-based
planner~\cite{gammell2015batch}. The Open Motion Planning Library (OMPL)
provides the benchmarking infrastructure~\cite{sucan2012ompl}. \rbf{} instead
constructs reusable volumetric context before query-local repair or optimization.

\section{Link Interval Envelopes}\label{sec:link_envelopes}

The separation of endpoint and link representations enables cross-revision
reuse. For a joint region
$I\subseteq\Q$, $E_k(I)$ bounds the trajectory of endpoint $k$ and is reusable
under an identity match, whereas $B_\ell(I)$ is derived under the
current descriptor and tested against the current scene:
\[
I \mapsto \{E_k(I)\} \mapsto \{B_\ell(I)\}.
\]
\rbf{} uses axis-aligned boxes as global forest cells and OBB regions only as
local finite-cover corridor witnesses. Only current-scene conservative
validation lets either region contribute to a path certificate.

\begin{assumption}[Collision model for certificates]
\label{ass:collision_model}
The certificate model covers collisions between active rounded links and a
finite union $\mathcal{O}$ of closed workspace obstacles represented by
axis-aligned bounding boxes (AABBs);
boundary contact is collision, and $I\subseteq\Q$ enforces joint limits. A
change to either the scene revision or descriptor $\delta$ invalidates every
scene-dependent box and edge label.
The semantic status $\mathrm{CertFree}$ denotes this conservative outcome; a
runtime label without the required proof carries no free-space claim.
Self-collision, attached bodies, grippers, and task constraints are outside
this model and would require additional conservative validation.
\end{assumption}

\paragraph{Numerical contract}
Soundness assumes outward-rounded endpoint and link envelopes, with all
numerical margins included in $\delta$. Here $\Ball_p(r)$ is the $p$-norm ball
of radius $r$; the AABB broadphase inflates by $\Ball_\infty(r_\ell)$, which contains the
Euclidean link-radius ball. The soundness results below therefore apply to an
implementation that enforces this numerical contract; all such results are
conditional on it.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\begin{definition}[Returned-path certificate status]
\label{def:path_witness}
Let $\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}$ denote the current scene revision, let
$\calO(\nu_{\mathrm{scene}})$ be its obstacle set, and fix descriptor $\delta$
and a continuous returned path $\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Q$. A retained
finite-cover witness $W=\bigl\{(J_j,R_j)\bigr\}_{j=1}^{m}$ consists of closed
parameter windows $J_j\subseteq[0,1]$ and regions $R_j\subseteq\Q$ satisfying
$[0,1]=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}J_j$ and $\gamma(J_j)\subseteq R_j$. Each retained
$R_j$ is either (i) an
axis-aligned box for which the current validator returns $\mathrm{CertFree}$,
(ii) an axis-aligned box that is the exact union of same-revision,
descriptor-matched $\mathrm{CertFree}$ boxes with a retained verified-tiling record,
or (iii) a complete \cspace{} OBB region for which the affine-zonotope validator
returns $\mathrm{CertFree}$ under the same scene revision and descriptor. The
path is certificate-backed
exactly when such a retained $W$ exists; otherwise it is validation-required.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Geometric Foundation}\label{sec:rewrite_geom}

For a body-frame shape $C_0$, let $T(q)x=R(q)x+t(q)$ map point $x$ to
workspace, with rotation $R(q)$ and translation $t(q)$, and let
$\mathcal{S}_I(C_0)$ denote its sweep over $q\in I$ and $\oplus$ the Minkowski
sum. For a capsule
$C=S\oplus\Ball_2(r)$ with center segment $S$, rigid motion gives
\[
\mathcal{S}_I(C)=\mathcal{S}_I(S)\oplus \Ball_2(r).
\]
Thus a rounded link can be bounded by first bounding the zero-radius center
segment and then lifting by the link radius. Let \(\Gamma_a(I)\) and
\(\Gamma_b(I)\) denote the exact workspace trajectories of a capsule link's
proximal and distal endpoints over \(I\). \rbf{} stores conservative endpoint
bounds in reusable records keyed by kinematics; the following proposition
converts them into whole-link envelopes.

\begin{samepage}
\begin{proposition}[Endpoint envelopes induce link interval envelopes]
\label{prop:endpoint_to_link_envelope}
Consider a rounded link with body-frame endpoints $a,b$,
$L=[a,b]\oplus \Ball_2(r)$, and any joint region $R\subseteq\Q$. If endpoint
envelopes $E_a(R)$ and $E_b(R)$ satisfy
$\Gamma_a(R)\subseteq E_a(R)$ and $\Gamma_b(R)\subseteq E_b(R)$, then
\[
\mathcal{S}_R(L)
\subseteq
\operatorname{conv}\bigl(E_a(R)\cup E_b(R)\bigr)\oplus \Ball_2(r).
\]
Consequently, any conservative outer representation of the right-hand side is a
valid link envelope for the whole joint region $R$.
\end{proposition}
\end{samepage}

\begin{proof}
For each $q\in R$, the moved center segment is the convex hull of its endpoints.
Hence the union of these segments lies in
\(\operatorname{conv}(\Gamma_a(R)\cup\Gamma_b(R))\subseteq
\operatorname{conv}(E_a(R)\cup E_b(R))\). Adding the fixed-radius ball then
yields the capsule sweep and the stated containment.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Conservative configuration-space box validation]
\label{cor:box_validation}
Let $I\subseteq\Q$ be an axis-aligned configuration-space box and let
$\{B_\ell(I)\}$ be conservative link interval envelopes for all active rounded links. If
$B_\ell(I)\cap\calO=\emptyset$ for every link $\ell$, then every configuration
$q\in I$ is collision-free with respect to the collision model in
\Cref{ass:collision_model}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By conservatism, each $B_\ell(I)$ contains the complete workspace sweep of link
$\ell$ over $I$. Its disjointness from $\calO$ therefore excludes the modeled
workspace-obstacle collision for every active link and every $q\in I$ under
\Cref{ass:collision_model}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Endpoint Interval Envelopes}\label{sec:rewrite_endpoint_env}

The cached primitive is an interval AABB (iAABB) enclosing the endpoint
trajectory $\Gamma_k(I)$ and shared by all link representations.
Affine-arithmetic interval forward kinematics (IFK-AA) uses one
Denavit--Hartenberg interval pass. Hierarchical interval forward kinematics
(HIFK) bisects $I$ to depth $d$ and hulls the child boxes, denoted HIFK-$d$.
Within this certificate model, Critical-sample bounds are filter-only and
ineligible for $\mathrm{CertFree}$; Q4 also reports Analytical and Monte Carlo
empirical references.
Fig.~\ref{fig:linkiaabb} summarizes the conversion.

\subsection{C-space OBB Endpoint Envelopes}\label{sec:obb_endpoint_env}

Axis-aligned boxes remain the global forest primitive; an OBB is local edge
evidence for a bridge or repair. Let \(q_c\) be its center, let the diagonal
matrix \(S\succ0\) scale the joints, let \(U\in\Real^{n\times m}\), $1\le m\le n$,
have orthonormal columns in scaled coordinates, and let
\(h\in\Real_{\ge0}^{m}\) contain the
half-widths. With \(A=S^{-1}U\operatorname{diag}(h)\) and
\(\Xi=\{\xi\in\Real^m:-1\le\xi_i\le1\}\), the joint-space region is
\[
R_{\mathrm{obb}}
=
\{\,q_c + A\xi \mid \xi\in\Xi\,\}.
\]
Here ``OBB'' denotes this affine image; it may be lower dimensional when
$m<n$, and the full-dimensional case has $m=n$.
For $R_{\mathrm{obb}}$, the correlated validator bounds each endpoint
trajectory using the outward-rounded affine-zonotope enclosure
$E_k(R_{\mathrm{obb}})$ specified below.
Certificate eligibility requires \(R_{\mathrm{obb}}\subseteq\Q\), verified
containment of the represented path window, and conservative validation of the
complete region; the fitted orientation alone provides no safety status.

\paragraph{Finite path cover}
A piecewise-linear candidate path $\gamma$ has an OBB witness only if finitely
many closed windows $J_\tau$ cover $[0,1]$, with
$\gamma(J_\tau)\subseteq R_\tau\subseteq\Q$, and every complete $R_\tau$ passes
the correlated endpoint/link test. Any uncovered window leaves the path
validation-required; sampled fallbacks may guide search but cannot establish
certificate status.

\begin{corollary}[OBB-region and finite-cover path soundness]
\label{cor:obb_corridor}
Fix a scene revision $\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}$ and descriptor $\delta$, and write
$\calO=\calO(\nu_{\mathrm{scene}})$. Let $\{R_\tau\}_{\tau=1}^{M}$ be a finite
family of joint-space OBB regions contained in $\Q$. Suppose that, for every
$\tau$, conservative affine-zonotope endpoint enclosures induce
conservative envelopes for all active links over the complete region $R_\tau$,
and that those link envelopes pass conservative separation tests against the
current-scene obstacles under the same revision and descriptor. Then
$R_\tau\subseteq\Cfree(\calO)$ for every $\tau$. Consequently, if closed
windows $J_\tau$ cover $[0,1]$ and a continuous path $\gamma$ satisfies
$\gamma(J_\tau)\subseteq R_\tau$ for every $\tau$, then
$\gamma([0,1])\subseteq\Cfree(\calO)$ under \Cref{ass:collision_model}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For each $\tau$, \Cref{prop:endpoint_to_link_envelope} and current-scene
separation give $R_\tau\subseteq\Cfree(\calO)$. Under the additional cover
hypothesis, every $t\in[0,1]$ belongs to some $J_\tau$, and hence
$\gamma(t)\in R_\tau\subseteq\Cfree(\calO)$. The path claim follows.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Correlated endpoint envelope}
For \(q=q_c+A\xi\), outward affine propagation gives
\[
E_k(R_{\mathrm{obb}})
=
c_k+\sum_{i=1}^{p} g_{k,i}\epsilon_i \oplus [-\rho_k,\rho_k].
\]
Here $p$ is the number of affine generators,
$c_k,g_{k,i}\in\Real^3$, $\epsilon_i\in[-1,1]$, and
$\rho_k\in\Real_{\ge0}^3$; $[-\rho_k,\rho_k]$ is componentwise and
\(\rho_k\) outwardly encloses the entire nonlinear remainder. A sampled
remainder is filter-only. For workspace direction \(d\in\mathbb{R}^3\), the
support of $E_k$ is
\[
h_{E_k}(d)=d^\top c_k+\sum_{i=1}^{p} |d^\top g_{k,i}|
          +\sum_{\mu=1}^3 |d_\mu|\rho_{k,\mu}.
\]
The rounded-link support bound is therefore
\[
\bar h_\ell(d)=
\max\{h_{E_a}(d),h_{E_b}(d)\}+r_\ell\|d\|_2.
\]
Coordinate directions provide the AABB broadphase; retained supports provide
the SupportHull/GJK test. OBB records remain scene-local typed-edge evidence
and do not become persistent LECT records or global forest nodes.

\subsection{Link-Envelope Representations}\label{sec:rewrite_link_env}

Endpoint records can be exported to multiple link representations without
changing the upstream kinematic source. For a serial-chain capsule link \(\ell\)
whose proximal and distal endpoint records are \(E_{\ell-1}(I)\) and
\(E_\ell(I)\),
$B_\ell(I)=B_\ell^\circ(I)\oplus\mathcal{R}(r_\ell)$, with
$B_\ell^\circ(I)$ bounding the centerline sweep and
\(\mathcal{R}(r_\ell)\supseteq\Ball_2(r_\ell)\) denoting a conservative
radius-lift set. AABB uses \(\Ball_\infty(r_\ell)\), while SupportHull uses the
Euclidean-ball support.

\paragraph{AABB envelope}
Each AABB envelope encloses the two endpoint envelopes in a single box and
expands it by the link radius:
\[
B_\ell(I) = \operatorname{bbox}\bigl(E_{\ell-1}(I) \cup E_\ell(I)\bigr)
\oplus \Ball_\infty(r_\ell).
\]
Here \(\operatorname{bbox}(\cdot)\) denotes the workspace AABB hull.

\paragraph{SupportHull envelope}
SupportHull stores support mappings for both endpoint enclosures and the link
radius. The default implementation uses endpoint AABBs; correlated OBB
corridors use the zonotope-plus-box records above. A bounded-work GJK narrow
phase uses outward obstacle inflation. If the iteration limit is reached without
a decision, the method returns $\mathrm{Unknown}$ rather than a free label.

\section{Lifelong Envelope Cache Tree}\label{sec:lect}

LECT supplies the cross-revision half of the reuse contract by indexing
obstacle-independent endpoint evidence with the dyadic joint-space boxes used
in forest construction. \rbf{} derives link envelopes under the current descriptor and
refreshes every scene-dependent label; graph connectivity and query outcomes
are never reused across scene revisions.

\subsection{Cache Key and Split Policy}\label{sec:lect_design}

LECT is a dynamically grown binary \(k\)-d tree over $\Q$; each node stores a
joint interval, split metadata, and geometry records.

\paragraph{Cache identity}
Replay requires exact matches of endpoint-record identity $\kappa_E$ and dyadic
address. The identity binds robot kinematics, endpoint representation, root
canonicalization, split policy, schema, and builder version, whereas $\delta$
binds link geometry and validation settings; only endpoint records persist.
LECT and the forest follow one deterministic midpoint split schedule, aligning
records with primitive cells without geometric rematching.

Formally, let $\kappa_E$ pass the endpoint-record gate above, fixing a common
root box, canonicalization, and split schedule $s_{\kappa_E}$. For a root-relative
child address $\alpha=(c_1,\ldots,c_d)$, $c_i\in\{0,1\}$, the shared decoder
maintains
\[
I_{\kappa_E}^{\mathrm{LECT}}(\alpha)
=I_{\kappa_E}^{\mathrm{forest}}(\alpha)=I_{\kappa_E}(\alpha).
\]
For endpoint $k$, each stored record $(\kappa_E,\alpha,k,E_k)$ satisfies the
record invariant
\[
\Gamma_k\!\left(I_{\kappa_E}(\alpha)\right)\subseteq E_k.
\]
Certificate-eligible coalesced nodes must retain an exact-union record and the
matched revision--descriptor label; they never become persistent cache keys.
Constituent boxes may leave the active graph only while that record remains. This
identity invariant, rather than geometric lookup, provides the interface
between cache replay and forest refinement.

\paragraph{Sparse interval staging}
Planning may stage a canonical dyadic interval at address $\alpha$ without
materializing all of its ancestors. If its endpoint record is absent, the record
is materialized under $(\kappa_E,\alpha)$ before current-scene validation.

\subsection{Runtime Use and Validation Semantics}

For a joint interval, LECT replays or materializes endpoint evidence, derives
link envelopes under $\delta$, and applies the current-scene validator. When
child endpoint records are hulled to form a parent endpoint enclosure, link
envelopes are re-derived for the parent joint cell and validated against the
current scene; child labels are not inherited. Missing or inconclusive evidence
yields $\mathrm{Unknown}$. Cache hits may change enclosure tightness and hence
the acceptance outcome, but not the meaning or validation obligations of any
returned status.

The cache-identity gate above controls replay eligibility; soundness additionally
requires decoded containment and fresh conservative scene validation.

\begin{proposition}[Sound replay of matched endpoint enclosures]
\label{prop:scene_safe_replay}
Let $I=I_{\kappa_E}(\alpha)\subseteq\Q$, and fix the current scene revision
$\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}$ and descriptor $\delta$. Suppose each required endpoint
record matches the current $\kappa_E$ and satisfies the record invariant above
at address $\alpha$. If the link envelopes derived under $\delta$ are
conservative and all pass conservative separation tests against
$\calO(\nu_{\mathrm{scene}})$, then
$I\subseteq\Cfree(\calO(\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}))$ under
\Cref{ass:collision_model}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The matched record invariant supplies every endpoint inclusion over $I$.
\Cref{prop:endpoint_to_link_envelope} therefore places each rounded-link sweep
inside its derived conservative envelope. Current-scene separation and
\Cref{cor:box_validation} give the stated inclusion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Storage and Revision Semantics}\label{sec:lect_storage}

Serialization retains compatible endpoint geometry and split metadata only;
link envelopes, labels, adjacency, and query outcomes are reconstructed per
revision.

\section{RapidBoxForest Construction}\label{sec:forest}

Fig.~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes the planning layer. Shared dyadic cell
identities let replay, batch validation, and refinement address the same joint
boxes. \rbf{} turns envelope validation and LECT replay into a query-reusable,
scene-specific structure comprising policy-accepted \cspace{} boxes and typed
corridor adjacencies.
Queries then use box membership and graph search. A box can support $W$ only
through current-scene conservative validation or a retained same-revision
exact-union witness.

A box is an axis-aligned joint interval
$B=[l^{(1)},u^{(1)}]\times\cdots\times[l^{(n)},u^{(n)}]\subseteq\Q$.
A \emph{policy-accepted box} has passed the configured scene-validation policy.
Under the stated geometric and numerical assumptions, conservative link-envelope
disjointness makes it eligible for \(\mathrm{CertFree}\); filter-only acceptance
yields a provisional region that cannot contribute to $W$. Rejected or
unresolved cells may be retained for refinement but are excluded from the
forest.

\subsection{Budgeted Construction Contract}\label{sec:forest_objective}

Construction returns a resource-bounded reusable region graph. Given the
joint-limit box $\Q$ and obstacle set $\calO$, the scene policy
$V_{\calO}(B;\delta)$ evaluates active-link envelopes for $B$ under descriptor
$\delta$ and has range
\[
\begin{split}
V_{\calO}(B;\delta)\in\{&\mathrm{CertFree},\mathrm{ProvisionalFree},\\[-1mm]
&\mathrm{Occupied},\mathrm{Unknown}\}.
\end{split}
\]
By definition of the conservative branch,
\[
V_{\calO}(B;\delta)=\mathrm{CertFree}
\ \Longrightarrow\ B\subseteq\Cfree(\calO).
\]
\(\mathrm{ProvisionalFree}\) is filter-only, \(\mathrm{Occupied}\) denotes a
policy rejection rather than a certificate of collision, and
\(\mathrm{Unknown}\) is inconclusive. The accepted region set of \(N\) boxes is
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal{F}=\bigl\{(B_i,\sigma_i):i=1,\ldots,N\bigr\},\\
B_i\subseteq\Q,\qquad \sigma_i\in
\{\mathrm{CertFree},\mathrm{ProvisionalFree}\}.
\end{gathered}
\]

Algorithm~\ref{alg:leaf_sweep_refine} states the certificate-producing branch
and therefore commits only boxes with semantic $\mathrm{CertFree}$ status.
Filter-only profiles may retain $\mathrm{ProvisionalFree}$ boxes for search, but
those boxes cannot contribute to $W$ without conservative revalidation. For a
same-revision family $\mathcal{G}$
with matching descriptor,
let $H=\operatorname{bbox}(\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{G}}B)$; the hull inherits this
label exactly when
\[
\operatorname{ExactUnion}(H,\mathcal{G})
\Longleftrightarrow H=\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{G}}B
\]
holds. Let $\varepsilon_c$ denote the connection tolerance, with
$\varepsilon_c\ge0$; it does not enter this predicate. Exact union is tested by
dyadic integer-range coverage, not floating-point volume equality, and is
enabled in Q1 and at the Q2 \rbf{} operating point but disabled in Q3. Let
$\mathcal{S}\subseteq\Q$ be a finite
anchor set fixed before queries; it affects admission priority, not
$V_{\calO}$ or certificate status. The reusable scene state
$(\mathcal{F},G_\cap)$ is certificate-eligible only with recorded revision,
descriptor, and exact-union provenance; query-specific typed edges are added
only during corridor construction.

For two boxes
\[
B_i=\prod_{d=1}^{n}[l_i^{(d)},u_i^{(d)}],\qquad
B_j=\prod_{d=1}^{n}[l_j^{(d)},u_j^{(d)}],
\]
\rbf{} treats the boxes as candidate-adjacent when their closed intervals in
every coordinate overlap or have a gap no larger than \(\varepsilon_c\):
\[
\max\{l_i^{(d)},l_j^{(d)}\}
\le
\min\{u_i^{(d)},u_j^{(d)}\}+\varepsilon_c,\qquad d=1,\ldots,n.
\]
Only exact overlap or contact directly yields a contained box corridor.
Tolerance-gap, segment, and repair edges are search aids; they support a
certificate only after conversion to a conservatively validated box corridor
or finite-cover OBB corridor.

\subsection{Build Pipeline}

Let $\mathcal{T}_L$ denote the LECT tree, and let
$G_\cap=(\mathcal{F},E_\cap)$ be the reusable exact-overlap/contact subgraph.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:leaf_sweep_refine} batch-validates a LECT-aligned frontier,
coalesces exact unions, and refines unresolved regions under an anchor-prioritized
budget. Its $\widehat V_{\calO}(I;\kappa_E,\delta)$ operation obtains or
materializes matched endpoint evidence, derives and validates current link
envelopes, and maps missing or inconclusive evidence to $\mathrm{Unknown}$.
It returns $\mathcal{F}$, $G_\cap$, and deferred frontier state; attachments and
typed transitions are query-local.

\subsection{Corridor Query and Certificate}
\label{sec:forest_graph}

The typed graph is a query-local overlay on $G_\cap$:
$G=(\mathcal{F},E_{\cap}\cup E_{\varepsilon}\cup E_s\cup
E_{\mathrm{obb}}\cup E_{\mathrm{rep}})$, comprising overlap/contact, tolerance
gap, candidate-segment, finite-cover OBB-corridor, and repair edges. Start/goal
attachments remain separate. Edge types record candidate origin, whereas
\Cref{def:path_witness} alone determines certificate status.

For a returned route, an overlap/contact transition contributes its incident
validated boxes and contained gateway; an OBB transition contributes its
retained finite cover. Other edges and endpoint attachments contribute no
witness until converted to a conservatively validated box corridor or
finite-cover OBB corridor. Concatenating the parameter windows constructs
$W$; certificate status requires full coverage without an unsupported transition.

A best-first traversal orders candidate routes heuristically. For overlap
routes, gateways lie in consecutive box intersections, and convexity keeps
their connecting segments within witnessed boxes.

\begin{lemma}[Box-corridor certificate]
\label{lem:corridor_certificate}
For scene revision $\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}$ and descriptor $\delta$, let
$\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cert}}$ contain boxes labeled $\mathrm{CertFree}$ against
$\calO(\nu_{\mathrm{scene}})$, each directly validated or retained as a
zero-gap exact union of such boxes under the same
$(\nu_{\mathrm{scene}},\delta)$ pair. Set
$R_{\mathrm{cert}}=\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cert}}}B$. If a continuous
path $\gamma:[0,1]\to\Q$ lies in $R_{\mathrm{cert}}$, then
$\gamma([0,1])\subseteq\Cfree(\calO(\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}))$ under
\Cref{ass:collision_model}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Directly validated boxes lie in $\Cfree$ by \Cref{cor:box_validation}; each
coalesced box is an exact union of such boxes under the same
$(\nu_{\mathrm{scene}},\delta)$. Hence
$R_{\mathrm{cert}}\subseteq\Cfree$, and the path inclusion follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Route construction from an overlap corridor]
\label{cor:route_construction}
Let $B_1,\ldots,B_m\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cert}}$, with
$q_s\in B_1$, $q_g\in B_m$, and gateways
$g_i\in B_i\cap B_{i+1}$. The polyline
$(q_s,g_1,\ldots,g_{m-1},q_g)$ lies in $R_{\mathrm{cert}}$ and is collision-free
under \Cref{ass:collision_model}. Replacing a route segment by a continuous
segment whose complete image is covered by a retained finite cover of validated
OBB regions preserves the conclusion. Any transition lacking a retained
validated-box or OBB-cover witness makes the returned route validation-required.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $g_0=q_s$ and $g_m=q_g$. For each $i=1,\ldots,m$, both endpoints of
$[g_{i-1},g_i]$ lie in the convex box $B_i$, so the complete polyline lies in
$R_{\mathrm{cert}}$ and \Cref{lem:corridor_certificate} applies. A validated
OBB replacement follows from \Cref{cor:obb_corridor}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Continuous collision freedom]
\label{thm:rbf_continuous_safety}
Under \Cref{ass:collision_model} and the numerical contract above, fix a scene
revision $\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}$ and descriptor $\delta$. For any continuous
returned path $\gamma:[0,1]\to\Q$ for which a conforming implementation retains
a witness $W$ satisfying \Cref{def:path_witness},
\[
    \forall t\in[0,1],\qquad
    \gamma(t)\in\Cfree(\calO(\nu_{\mathrm{scene}})).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Cross-revision path soundness]
\label{cor:cross_revision_path}
After an obstacle revision from $\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}$ to
$\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}'$ that preserves $\kappa_E$, fix the current descriptor
$\delta$. Suppose every replayed endpoint record matches $\kappa_E$ and
satisfies the record invariant, and every witness region is either directly
revalidated against $\calO(\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}')$ under $\delta$ or newly
constructed by a verified exact-union operation on
$\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}'$-matched, $\delta$-matched $\mathrm{CertFree}$ boxes. The
certificate-backed returned path is then collision-free for
every $t\in[0,1]$ in the revised scene.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Matched replay followed by fresh current-scene validation makes each primitive
box sound by \Cref{prop:scene_safe_replay}. Directly revalidated OBB regions are
sound by \Cref{cor:obb_corridor}, and a newly verified exact union preserves the
inclusion of its current-revision constituents. Thus the retained witness
satisfies \Cref{def:path_witness} for $(\nu_{\mathrm{scene}}',\delta)$, and
\Cref{thm:rbf_continuous_safety} applies to its complete cover.
\end{proof}

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

\Cref{thm:rbf_continuous_safety} establishes continuous-path collision freedom
from conservative finite-region coverage; the evaluation does not attempt to
re-establish this guarantee by sampling. Instead, it addresses four questions
about operating cost and planning behavior. Q1 characterizes a fixed warm-cache
Shelf+KUKA LBR iiwa (Shelf+IIWA) profile and compound profile controls. Q2
provides path-length context against roadmap, sampling, and convex-region
baselines. Q3 characterizes selected in-sample profiles on three robots, and Q4
isolates endpoint- and link-envelope trade-offs.
Fig.~\ref{fig:tro_shelf_scene} introduces the fixed Shelf scene.
The Q2 baseline couples iterative regional inflation with Graphs of Convex Sets
(IRIS/GCS).
Table~\ref{tab:evaluation_map} maps each empirical question to its evidence and
reuse mechanism.

\noindent\emph{Protocol and metrics.}
Experiments were run on Ubuntu~22.04 with an Intel Core i5-12600KF\@. OMPL
planners were single-threaded; timings imported from archived runs without
host-load metadata are treated as descriptive.

Build denotes post-initialization reusable construction time. For \rbf{}, this
metric includes
evidence access or materialization, scene labeling, graph and anchor construction,
and shortcut generation, but excludes snapshot creation/opening; PRM endpoints are
preloaded. \onlineq{} denotes per-query work. Timings exclude simplification.
Successful runtime completion and the subsequent sampled check are empirical
gates. Each saved polyline was subsequently densified to at most $0.01$~rad
between samples and collision-checked;
this check was untimed. A failed gate excludes the record and can change reported
completion, but no sampled gate establishes $W$ or supports
\Cref{thm:rbf_continuous_safety}. The results API and archives omit a complete
witness $W$, so no per-path certificate-coverage rate is reported; experiments
measure cost, completion, and path quality. The theorem applies only to a final
path with retained $W$; repair or postprocessing requires reconstructing $W$.

In Q1, Batch timestamp denotes a query's within-run completion time in a jointly
scheduled five-query run (40 observations nested within eight runs). Q2 reports
eight seeds per query, and each Q3 condition contains 100
queries nested within ten scenes. Q3 uses
\[
\mathrm{Task/q}=\left(T_{\mathrm{adapt}}^{\mathrm{wall}}+
\sum_{i=1}^{N_q}T_{\mathrm{query},i}^{\mathrm{internal}}\right)/N_q.
\]
Here, $N_q$ is the batch size; the two terms are scene-adaptation wall time and
internal query time. For accounting only, the reuse projections use unrounded
metric values and add $\mathrm{Build}/K$ to a fixed Task/q value; Shelf $K>5$ and
random-scene $K>10$ are extrapolated. Tables report descriptive
medians and interquartile ranges, not confidence intervals. Let $L$ denote
joint-space path length for a saved scene--query instance $\lambda$. We report
$L/L_{\mathrm{ref},\lambda}$, where $L_{\mathrm{ref},\lambda}$ is the empirical
minimum computed from the same data and pooled over the fixed methods and
checkpoints before selection; it is neither a known optimum nor a held-out
reference. Shelf uses the corresponding per-query reference. In
Fig.~\ref{fig:tro_shelf_cross_tradeoff}, $\bar L$ and $\bar L_{\mathrm{ref}}$
denote the displayed mean path length and its empirical reference minimum,
respectively.

\noindent\emph{Profile inheritance and cache accounting.}
The Q1 baseline and Q2 \rbf{} operating point use a read-only depth-23 LECT and
a 100-box admission cap (d23/b100). Q1 controls follow registered,
profile-specific cache assignments rather than a common cache treatment:
HIFK-5 is the no-cache contrast; Critical-sample replays its matching d23 cache;
and Link-AABB and SupportHull-only retain IFK-AA+d23. Q3 inherits the \rbf{}
build/query backend, collision checker,
postprocessor, and data-integrity protocol, but not the Shelf anchors, b100 cap,
or Shelf cache root. Its six robot--difficulty profiles use robot-local roots:
IIWA and Panda replay depth-23 and depth-20 records, respectively, whereas UR5
materializes endpoint evidence. For cache-backed profiles, Warm Build includes
lookup, reads, and demand paging but excludes snapshot creation/opening and the
one-time d23 prewarm (16.8 million records, a 12.74~GiB serialized cache, and
1008.6~s); peak resident memory is not reported. The Q1 controls are compound
profiles that vary endpoint source, replay, or link representation. Q4
separately measures endpoint construction and isolated link-envelope
operations.

\subsection{Q3: Random Scenes Across Robot Models}
\label{sec:exp_generalization}

\emph{Dataset and selection.}
Q3 uses a frozen catalog of three robots, ten seeds per difficulty, and ten
queries per scene: 60 scenes spanning the Medium and Hard settings and 600 saved
scene--query instances per method. For each robot--seed pair, the Hard obstacle
prefix strictly extends the Medium prefix; queries are shared, and direct
start--goal segments are obstructed. The obstacle prefixes and one \rbf{} profile
per robot--difficulty condition were tuned and evaluated on this same catalog.
Each reported profile completed all 100 instances and was registered
from the full-completion candidates after joint inspection of Task/q and path
length on that catalog. Q3 is therefore descriptive and in-sample, not a
held-out generalization test.

\emph{Compared methods.}
\rbf{} reuses one query-agnostic forest for each ten-query scene. PRM builds one
endpoint-preloaded cumulative roadmap per scene; RRT-Connect and BIT* are
query-local. The full-success RRT-Connect and PRM curves retain a new point only
after an aggregate path-length-ratio improvement of at least 1\%.
Table~\ref{tab:tro-random-summary}
selects BIT*'s fastest per-instance checkpoint whose path length is within 8\%

uses the 1\% display rule.

\emph{Results.}
Under these method-specific, in-sample selections, the reported \rbf{} median

\subsection{Discussion and Scope}

Three components form one algorithmic interface: canonical dyadic addresses
couple endpoint replay with forest refinement; exact-union coalescing compresses
the current graph without enlarging the certified free set; and typed box and OBB
transitions compose corridor witnesses. The dyadic cache hierarchy and
region-graph admission remain separately controlled: cache depth bounds replay
addresses, the admission cap limits evaluated frontier cells, and anchor
coverage affects only frontier-cell priority. Primitive keys remain unchanged by
revision-local unions. Changing endpoint
construction changes $\kappa_E$, and changing link representation changes
$\delta$, but neither changes certificate-status semantics.

Candidate generation is outside the certificate condition. A future, unevaluated
integration could expose the validator as an external checker for sampling- or
optimization-based planners and learned generators, including
vision--language--action (VLA) models such as OpenVLA and world-model--action
(WMA) architectures such as
\mbox{UnifoLM-WMA-0}~\cite{kim2024openvla,unitree2025unifolmwma}.
Such an interface could reuse endpoint evidence, but its end-to-end latency and
amortization remain to be measured. In this integration, the upstream planner or
model would generate candidates. \rbf{} would retain a complete finite cover of
regions validated for the current scene or flag unsupported windows for repair
or rejection. Under \Cref{ass:collision_model}, only a fully covered candidate
would be certificate-backed under \Cref{def:path_witness} and meet the premise
of \Cref{thm:rbf_continuous_safety}. Developing this interface requires
mapping learned action chunks to continuous joint-space paths,
synchronizing perceptual scene revisions, extending the conservative validator
to task-relevant collision models, and measuring end-to-end checking latency.

\rbf{} targets repeated queries for a fixed robot under compatible scene
revisions. Q2 reports selector-conditioned path quality, Q3 reports selected
in-sample profiles, and Q1 and Q4 characterize compound cache profiles and
isolated envelope mechanisms, respectively. The safety result establishes
geometric collision freedom only for paths with a retained finite-cover witness
as defined in \Cref{def:path_witness}, under a fixed scene revision and
\Cref{ass:collision_model}. The reported profiles are not evidence of numerical-
contract conformance or per-path certificate coverage. The result does not cover
dynamics, tracking error, or obstacles that move during execution.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

\rbf{} turns obstacle-independent kinematic bounds into reusable planning
state. A shared dyadic identity maps each endpoint record to a primitive cell of
the rebuilt region graph; compositional corridor verification combines
current-scene witnesses, and typed transitions expose repair interfaces. The budgeted
builder refines canonical cells and coalesces only exact dyadic unions,
preserving the certified free set while reducing the revision-local graph.
Under the stated collision and numerical assumptions, the resulting
finite-cover certificate guarantees collision freedom at every point of a
certificate-backed continuous path. The cross-revision conclusion holds only
under the matched-replay and fresh-validation conditions of
\Cref{cor:cross_revision_path}. On Shelf+IIWA, the
warm profile completed all 40 tasks, and its median warm Build time was
0.171~s. At the method-specific, in-sample operating points, the selected
\rbf{} profiles have median normalized path-length ratios below the
corresponding RRT-Connect medians in all six conditions and below the PRM and
BIT* medians in five. With Task/q held fixed, the projected
$\mathrm{Build}/K+\mathrm{Task/q}$ metric is 69.6--79.7\% lower at $K=10$ than
at $K=1$; this arithmetic comparison is not a measured cross-revision speedup.
The analysis establishes the conditional soundness of the cross-revision reuse
contract,
while the experiments characterize operating costs and selector-conditioned
path-length outcomes. Measuring end-to-end speedup across scene revisions and
evaluating whether the certificate interface can serve as a low-latency checker
for conventional planners and VLA- or WMA-based candidate generators remain
future work.
