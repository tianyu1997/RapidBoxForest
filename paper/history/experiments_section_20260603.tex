\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

This section evaluates \rbf{} as a multi-query planning system under the
current evaluation protocol. Returned paths are compared only after the
implemented query pipeline has completed its own post-processing and strict
final audit.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The main evidence consists of the current Shelf+IIWA leaf-refine ablation, the
link-envelope representation sweep, and the dynamic-update capability
comparison. A reduced random-scene study across IIWA, UR5, and Panda and a
compact Shelf+IIWA cross-algorithm sanity check are also reported for
completeness.

\paragraph{Reporting Protocol.}
Planner success in this paper means strict path-audit success under the current
query pipeline. Cache reuse is allowed only at the envelope-materialization
layer: target builds still regrow and revalidate scene-specific boxes, and warm
rows are never credited for persisted free-space labels. For the Shelf+IIWA
baseline warm rows, the external d23 cache is read-only during evaluation and
active-database backfill is disabled.

\paragraph{Fixed Configuration.}
The RBF planning rows use canonical IFK\_AA endpoint evidence, the reported
validation and final-audit policy, and the adopted link-envelope family
\{LinkIAABB, KDOP26, SupportHull\}. The Shelf+IIWA ablation uses the current
leaf-refine protocol: a virtual leaf sweep from depth 8 to 14 followed by
restricted depth-28 refinement. Its baseline warm rows use the read-only d23
external cache, while non-baseline comparison rows are run without external
cache replay or active endpoint-evidence caching unless explicitly stated.

\paragraph{Platform and Scenes.}
Planner rows use the worker count recorded in each result artifact. The
Shelf+IIWA leaf-refine baseline uses eight worker threads and reports an
explicit single-thread ablation row; the standalone link-envelope representation
microbenchmark uses the batch parallelism recorded in its result artifact.
Shelf+IIWA uses the canonical Marcucci scene with five fixed query pairs. The
random-scene benchmark uses balanced easy/medium/hard scenes for IIWA,
UR5, and Panda under the staged random-scene anytime protocol.

\Cref{fig:marcucci_scene,fig:tro_leaf_refine_tradeoff,tab:tro_leaf_refine_ablation}
summarize the Shelf+IIWA leaf-refine ablation. The d23 SupportHull baseline at
the box200 point is the fastest audited row, while the box500/d24 point removes
all segment-edge contribution and shortens the audited route at higher planning
cost. The time decomposition shows that this trade-off is dominated by the
restricted refinement budget after the leaf sweep; the connector is significant
but no longer the primary source of path quality. The no-cache SupportHull row
remains strict-audit successful but is slower and produces a longer route under
the same box200 refinement budget. The no-cache AABB row is close in runtime
and path length, but uses slightly more segment length; the defensible
representation conclusion from this optimized shelf row is therefore not
universal SupportHull dominance over AABB. The robust mechanism evidence is d23
evidence replay, anchor-guided restricted refinement, and parallel virtual leaf
validation. Disabling priority-anchor roots fails the two shelf transitions
that cross the narrowest region, identifying anchor-root refinement as a
required mechanism rather than a cosmetic tuning parameter.

\input{sbf_old/generated/tab_tro_shelf_cross_algorithm.tex}

\Cref{tab:tro_shelf_cross_algorithm} gives a compact cross-algorithm check on
the same Shelf+IIWA task set. The updated SBF leaf-refine row succeeds on all
15 audited queries and is far faster than the PRM and BIT* incumbent traces under
their cumulative accounting. RRTConnect also succeeds on all queries and is
slightly faster as a one-shot query planner, while the common per-query length
column favors the SBF leaf-refine row on this shelf slice. This table is
therefore not used to claim one-shot latency dominance over RRTConnect; its role
is to separate the explicit audited box-region graph behavior from single-query
sampling baselines. A reduced one-seed Shelf+IIWA IRIS-NP+GCS diagnostic with an
eight-region prefix took 121.2~s and produced no audited solution on the five
queries, so it is not treated as a competitive shelf row under the current
configuration.

The multi-robot random-scene benchmark is a staged anytime generalization study,
not the Shelf+IIWA leaf-refine protocol. It reports IIWA, UR5, and Panda across
easy, medium, and hard settings using per-robot p18 warm caches and
scene-specific staged SBF builds. This experiment is used to check that the
audit and cache machinery run across robots and random scenes, while preserving
the anytime structure of the older paper presentation. In
\cref{fig:tro_random_reduced_tradeoff}, SBF high-budget points are curve
endpoints: they charge all preceding stages and mainly diagnose whether more
box-forest budget buys path quality. They are not the fast random-scene
baseline. Accordingly, \cref{tab:tro_random_reduced} reports the first
strict-audit-successful SBF seed stage by difficulty; this removes the
misleading impression that the 34~s high-stage endpoint is the baseline
configuration. The reduced run passes strict audit across all nine
robot--difficulty tasks for every reported row, but PRM, BIT*, and RRTConnect
solve these one-shot random queries at much lower charged time than the staged
SBF protocol, and the reduced IRIS-NP+GCS prefix is also faster than the SBF
seed stage on this small random slice. The result should therefore not be read
as a direct runtime extrapolation of the Shelf+IIWA leaf-refine baseline or as a
claim that staged SBF dominates single-query sampling planners on random
scenes.

\subsection{Mechanism Studies: Link Envelopes}\label{sec:exp_mechanisms}

\input{sbf_old/generated/tab_tro_link_envelopes.tex}

\paragraph{Link-envelope chain modes.}
\Cref{tab:tro_link_envelopes} extends the standalone link-envelope
microbenchmark beyond independently built LinkIAABB, KDOP26, and SupportHull
records. The staged chains run the cheap AABB filter first and invoke KDOP26 or
SupportHull only for boxes that survive the earlier stage. Across the 2400-box
fixed-width corpus, only 28.3\% of boxes reach the tighter second stage, so the
AABB--KDOP26 and AABB--SupportHull chains average 1.27~$\mu$s and
1.25~$\mu$s per box, compared with 1.94~$\mu$s for standalone KDOP26 and
1.77~$\mu$s for standalone SupportHull. The full AABB--KDOP26--SupportHull
cascade averages 1.85~$\mu$s per box. \Cref{tab:tro_appendix_rbf_only_sweep}
compresses the same artifact into one aggregate summary view.

\input{sbf_old/generated/tab_tro_rbf_only_sweep.tex}

\paragraph{Envelope sweep.}
\Cref{tab:tro_appendix_rbf_only_sweep} gives a compact cross-width summary of
the representation study. LinkIAABB remains the cheapest standalone
representation at 0.68~$\mu$s per box and 384~B payload. Standalone KDOP26 and
SupportHull increase cost to 1.94~$\mu$s and 1.77~$\mu$s, respectively. The
AABB-first chains recover much of that overhead: AABB--KDOP26 averages
1.27~$\mu$s per box at 854.8~B, AABB--SupportHull averages 1.25~$\mu$s at
619.4~B, and all three chain rows escalate beyond the AABB stage on only 28.3\%
of the boxes in the fixed-width corpus.


\subsection{Dynamic-Update Capability Study}\label{sec:exp_dynamic_updates}

\Cref{tab:tro_dynamic_rebuild} reports the revised dynamic-update accounting
for the leaf-sweep--RRT planner. The protocol uses saved constrained random
AABB scenes sampled inside the Exp.~4 d23 canonical inverse-root domain, so the
same scenes can be replayed for both incremental updates and warm target-scene
rebuilds. Insertions scan the live free-box forest against the added obstacles
and record invalidated boxes under those obstacles. Deletions query the shallow
leaf-sweep collision-domain cache, promote domains whose blocker set
disappears, and do not re-run envelope validation for those promoted domains.
Insertions use a local relative-depth leaf-sweep refill inside invalidated boxes
instead of a bounded RRT/FFB repair stage; Table~VIII uses $k=0$, i.e., it
rechecks the invalidated leaf without descending deeper.

\input{sbf_old/generated/tab_tro_dynamic_rebuild.tex}

The result separates two qualitatively different regimes. For obstacle
deletions, no accepted free box is invalidated; the blocker cache exposes about
7.1--8.1k candidate shallow domains per transition and the incremental update is
about 79--97$\times$ faster than a fresh warm rebuild while preserving all
eight queries. For obstacle insertions, the method correctly invalidates
hundreds of free boxes and locally sweeps the invalidated domains. Four of
eight insertion runs per transition require the final endpoint/segment fallback,
but the total incremental update remains about 17--19$\times$ faster than a warm
rebuild and all audited queries succeed. This supports the intended use of
segment connectivity as a last-resort recovery mechanism rather than the
primary update operator.
